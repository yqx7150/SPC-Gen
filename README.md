# SPC-Gen: Synthetic PET and CT Data using Generative Model in Projection Domain

## Introduction
In the field of nuclear medicine and medical imaging artificial intelligence, high-quality, large-scale, and physically consistent PET/CT datasets remain extremely scarce. PET imaging involves radioactive tracers, radiation exposure, high costs, and strict clinical constraints, while CT imaging is limited by radiation dose and scanner availability. Most existing generative methods for PET/CT only work in the image domain, ignoring the physical information in the projection domain, leading to anatomically plausible but physically inconsistent synthetic data, which severely limits their application in quantitative clinical analysis and high-precision AI model training.To address these challenges, we propose SPC-Gen, a large-scale synthetic PET/CT dataset generated by a projection-domain latent diffusion model. SPC-Gen explicitly preserves the physical characteristics of PET photon counting statistics and CT X-ray attenuation, maintains the intrinsic correspondence between projection domain and image domain, and covers a variety of tracers, anatomical regions, lesion types, and paired/unpaired data configurations. It provides a standardized, reusable, and physically reliable data resource for data-driven medical imaging research.This repository fully opens the SPC-Gen synthetic dataset, real clinical reference data, projection-domain generative model code, complete training and inference pipeline, and downstream task validation code, supporting one-stop reproduction, secondary development, and direct application in clinical AI scenarios.

## Key Features
Projection-domain generation paradigm directly models PET sinogram and CT projection data, retains physical imaging properties, avoids information loss caused by image-domain-only generation, and supports standard clinical reconstruction algorithms such as OSEM. Ultra-large-scale high-resolution data contains more than 50,000 pairs of projection-image slices, with a unified resolution of 512×512, covering single-tracer unpaired and dual-tracer paired data. Diverse tracer and anatomical coverage supports common clinical tracers: ¹⁸F‑FDG, ¹⁸F‑DOPA, ⁶⁸Ga‑PSMA; covers brain, trunk, abdomen, chest and other anatomical regions. Strict physical consistency and authenticity is evaluated by KID, FID, KL divergence and other indicators, showing that the distribution is highly consistent with real data; instance-level verification demonstrates high fidelity, diversity, and structural reliability. Strong clinical downstream usability can effectively support PET lesion segmentation, CT lesion segmentation, low-dose CT denoising, dual-tracer separation and fusion, and other clinical AI tasks. Standardized open ecosystem provides data in DICOM, .npy, .mat formats, complete code environment, configuration files, and pre-trained weights, making it ready to use out of the box.

## Model Architecture
The SPC-Gen generative framework is built on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) and VQ‑GAN to form an end-to-end projection-domain data generation pipeline, which first uses VQ‑GAN to map high-dimensional projection data into a compact discrete latent space, significantly reducing computational costs, and adopts a UNet architecture integrated with residual blocks and cross-attention layers as the core diffusion backbone; during the training process, the forward diffusion stage gradually injects Gaussian noise into real projection data, while the reverse diffusion stage relies on a denoising network to learn noise estimation and removal, enabling the generation of physically consistent projection data that can be directly reconstructed into clinical images via standard medical reconstruction pipelines. Furthermore, the entire model is trained with a low learning rate of 2.0×10⁻⁶ and a long diffusion schedule of 1000 steps to ensure stable convergence, high-fidelity data generation, and excellent reproducibility in large-scale dataset construction.
